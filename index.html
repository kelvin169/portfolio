01 Harbor A calmer operating board for berth assignment and shift handover. Product 2025

North Sea Dispatch · 2025

Harbor

A calmer operating board for berth assignment and shift handover.

Role
Lead product design
Timeline
11 months
Practice
Product
41%Fewer missed berths
6 minShift handover
4Night shifts shadowed

Problem

Dispatchers ran three radios, a spreadsheet, and a wall of paper slips. Handover between shifts dropped berth assignments, especially after midnight when the tide window was short.

Approach

I sat four night shifts before opening Figma. The board became a single tide-aware timeline. Paper slips turned into durable cards with a drag language the crew already used on the wall. Radio traffic stayed; the screen stopped competing with it.

Outcome

Shift handover fell from 18 minutes to 6. Missed berths dropped 41% in the first season. The night crew asked to keep a paper backup, so the printout shipped as part of the product, not a workaround.

02 Kindling A climate lesson teachers can run without a login wall. Learning 2024

City of Aarhus · 2024

Kindling

A climate lesson teachers can run without a login wall.

Role
Product design & prototyping
Timeline
7 months
Practice
Learning
140Classrooms in term one
0Accounts required
22 minAverage lesson

Problem

The city’s climate curriculum lived in a PDF and a forgotten platform. Teachers would not create accounts, and the children needed something they could touch between slides.

Approach

We designed a 22-minute lesson around wooden blocks and one shared screen. No student accounts. The teacher’s view is a paper-sized script with three prompts and a timer that does not scold.

Outcome

140 classrooms ran Kindling in the first term. Average lesson time landed at 22 minutes, which was the point. The city retired the login platform for this age group.

Stillroom

A steep timer that refuses to become a wellness brand.

Role
Design & engineering
Timeline
4 months
Practice
Product

800Quiet release

Problem

Tea software wanted streaks, scores, and a shop. The people who actually keep a stillroom wanted one kiln, one steep, and a note they could find next week.

Approach

One screen. A ceramic-colored clock, a leaf, a line for the note. No feed, no account, no recommendations. I wrote it as a small local app so the note stays on the device.

Outcome

Released quietly to a list of 800. The only metric we kept was whether people returned to yesterday’s note. They did, about three times a week.

04 Ledger & Light Commerce for shops that still ring a bell when a book arrives. Systems 2023

Six independent bookshops · 2023

Ledger & Light

Commerce for shops that still ring a bell when a book arrives.

Role
Design systems & product
Timeline
9 months
Practice
Systems
6Shops on one catalog
50%Fewer lost special orders
1Card still under the till

Problem

Six shops shared a wholesaler and nothing else. Online orders were a shared inbox. Special orders lived on cards under the till, and those cards were the soul of the place.

Approach

We kept the card. The system photographs it, files the customer, and prints a shelf slip in the shop’s own type. A shared catalog sits behind the counter, never on a customer-facing kiosk.

Outcome

Special-order losses dropped by half. Staff stopped retyping the inbox. Each shop kept its bell, its handwriting, and its own front-of-house voice.

05 Night Market Wayfinding for a market that only exists after dark. Identity 2023

Night Market

Wayfinding for a market that only exists after dark.

Role
Identity & environmental graphics
Timeline
5 months
Practice
Identity
4Gates, not a maze key
1 wordPer lantern
2Seasons on the same mark

Problem

Maps failed because the market moves. Stalls change lanes with the weather. Visitors arrived with a daytime map and got lost in a place that had already rearranged itself.

Approach

Lanterns became the sign system: color for the lane, a single word for the trade, hung low enough to read in rain. The printed map became a small card that only names the gates.

Outcome

Stewards reported fewer lost visitors on opening weekend, and the lanterns were reused the next season without a redesign. The identity is the light, not a logo wall.

Problem

The cooperative inherited an industrial SCADA screen. Growers ignored the alarms and kept paper logs, because the screen could not say “the ferns looked tired.”

Approach

We recast the dashboard as a bench notebook: morning reading, a dial, a line for the eye’s judgment. Alarms became a single margin note. Sensors stayed; the voice changed.

Outcome

Log completion went from sporadic to daily across twelve houses. The cooperative kept the paper page and files a photo of it beside the sensor trace.

About

Tools people can trust with their hands.

I started in print at Studio Mare, moved into product at Northwind, and now work independently with harbor masters, teachers, booksellers, and growers. The brief is usually the same: a tool that has to survive a real shift, not a demo.

I stay close to the work. That means night shifts, shop floors, and classrooms before any screen. I also write the interface in code when a prototype needs to be the thing people argue with.

  1. 01
    Spend time where the work happens.

    A conference-room interview is a rumor. The night shift, the till, the greenhouse bench — that is the brief.

  2. 02
    Make the quiet path the default.

    Most people are tired when they meet the software. The obvious action should not require a tour.

  3. 03
    Leave something they can hold.

    A printout, a card, a paper slip. Digital tools earn trust when they meet the physical habit already in the room.

Notes

From the studio

March 2026The night shift is the real stakeholderIf the person who closes the building cannot use it, the product is a slide.

On Harbor, the day team loved the first concept. The night team did not look at it. They were watching a tide window and three radios. We threw out the dashboard and sat two more shifts before drawing again.

The lesson was not “talk to users.” It was narrower: the stakeholder who is tired, interrupted, and accountable for a physical mistake outranks the stakeholder who presents the work.

November 2025Stop designing the empty state firstEmpty states are a designer's comfort. Full states are the product.

Kindling’s first prototype was a beautiful blank classroom. Teachers never see a blank classroom. They see thirty names, a broken projector, and twelve minutes until the bell.

We started over from a full roster and a half-finished lesson. The empty state became a single line at the bottom, written last.

June 2025A printout is an interfaceIf people keep a paper copy, design the paper. Do not call it a workaround.

The harbor crew asked to keep their slips. Ledger & Light still rings a bell and writes a card when a book arrives. Pretending those habits are legacy is how software gets ignored.

I now spec the print layout in the same file as the screen: type size, where the pencil lands, what survives a coffee ring.
